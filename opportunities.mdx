An **opportunity** is a match: one of your [buyers](/buyers) fits another broker's live [listing](/properties). This is the buyer side of the network — the platform finding you inventory for the clients you already have.

## Read an opportunity

Each opportunity card shows:

- The **match score** (how well the listing fits the buyer's brief).
- The listing's **price, type, beds**, and area.
- The **sub-agent fee** the listing broker is offering — both the **percentage** and the **estimated amount** (for example, `0.5% · ≈£1,500`).
- Whether you've **already sent interest** on it.

Open one to see the full **match breakdown** — price, location, type, beds, size, and amenities, side by side (buyer wants → property has) — plus the listing broker and their trust signals.

## Express interest

When an opportunity is worth pursuing:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the opportunity and review the breakdown">
    Check the match and the fee.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click Express interest">
    Optionally add a short note for the listing broker (for example, "cash buyer, can view this week").
  </Step>
  <Step title="A deal thread opens">
    This creates a [deal](/deals) with the listing broker. Buyer identity stays private until the deal progresses.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Once you've sent it, the opportunity reads **Interest sent — a deal thread is open** and takes you to the deal. If that interest doesn't go anywhere you can **Express interest again**.
</Note>

## From your Loop CRM

If you've connected [Loop](/data-connections), Opportunities also surfaces matches marked **From your Loop CRM**. These pair **your own CRM buyers** with other brokers' live listings — the network working the buyers already in your book.

Open one and the match is shown as evidence rather than a score. Each requirement says where it came from: **Area the buyer gave**, **Inferred from their notes**, or **BrokerBuddy read this from your Loop notes**. You can see what the buyer actually said and judge the fit yourself, instead of trusting a number.

Your buyer's identity stays private: the other broker never sees who they are unless a connection forms.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the match">
    Review the listing and why your buyer matches it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pursue">
    Pursuing re-checks your buyer's live consent, then sends a **connection request** to the listing broker. Both sides stay anonymous until you connect, and a [deal](/deals) opens.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Undo, if you are quick">
    Changed your mind? **Undo** shortly after cancels the request and removes the buyer's contact details. Leave it too long and you'll see *the owner may have already responded* — at that point it stands.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Only buyers who have consented to property matching in your CRM appear here, and their personal details aren't stored until you pursue.
</Note>

There is a **daily cap on connection requests**. Reach it and you'll see *You've reached today's connection limit — try again tomorrow*. It exists to keep the network a place where requests mean something, rather than something to blanket.

A listing that has since come off the market can't be pursued, and the card will say so.

## Dismiss an opportunity

Not a fit? **Dismiss** it and it won't show again. You can still find the buyer under [Buyers](/buyers). If an interest you sent doesn't go through, you can dismiss the opportunity or express interest again.
